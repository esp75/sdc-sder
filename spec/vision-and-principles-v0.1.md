## Purpose

Each mission has a physical lifecycle and can add to congestion or collision risk. Public decisions need a way to compare stated debris-management plans with observable outcomes, while recognizing uncertainty and allowing errors to be corrected. This document proposes two connected public-interest instruments:

- **Space Debris Exposure Rating (SDER):** a proposed assessment framework for describing a mission's contribution to, and management of, debris exposure across its lifecycle. An eventual rating would require a tested method, disclosed assumptions, independent review and appeal rights. No official SDER rating exists today.
- **Space Debris Credit (SDC):** a proposed evidence-based unit or record for a qualifying, independently verified reduction in debris-related risk or removal of debris. Its eligibility, unit of account, counterfactual and possible economic use remain open research questions. No SDC has been issued, sold or retired under this framework.

The vision is to make the consequences of space activity visible, comparable where scientifically defensible, and attributable to responsible actors. Measurement is a prerequisite for policy, procurement or economic incentives; it does not itself decide which policy instrument is appropriate.

## A simple model

1. **Describe the mission and its lifecycle.** Record what was launched, its operating environment, the disposal plan, evidence available and who is responsible for supplying it.
2. **Assess exposure with uncertainty.** Test proposed indicators against current science and disclose missing or disputed information. Do not turn a sparse dataset into a precise-looking score.
3. **Verify a qualifying improvement separately.** If a future SDC method is adopted, confirm that the claimed reduction or cleanup happened, that it is additional to the defined baseline, and that the same outcome has not been counted twice.

For example, a mission operator might document a disposal plan and later provide evidence of its actual end-of-life outcome. A future SDER method could use those facts, together with orbit and mission context, to describe exposure and uncertainty. A later cleanup activity would be evaluated on its own evidence. Neither activity earns a present-day rating or credit merely by submitting a claim.

## Principles for SDER development

1. **Lifecycle scope.** Consider design, launch, operations, contingencies, disposal and the period of residual exposure. The exact system boundary must be published with each method version.
2. **Observable evidence.** Distinguish operator statements, independently observed data, verified records and modeled estimates. Publish evidence requirements before assessments begin.
3. **Scientific uncertainty.** Report model assumptions, confidence ranges or qualitative uncertainty, and sensitivity to key parameters. Do not imply a single score is more certain than its inputs.
4. **Missing data.** State what is missing and whether the result is withheld, provisional or bounded. Never silently substitute favorable assumptions for absent evidence.
5. **Comparability with limits.** Compare only like cases under a published method. Avoid league tables until coverage, peer review, correction and appeal processes are functioning.
6. **Independence and due process.** Separate data provision, method setting, assessment and appeals. The originator, and any organisation affiliated with a contributor, may participate as an implementer but cannot assess or certify itself, chair permanently or veto a method.
7. **Versioned decisions.** Preserve the method version, evidence snapshot, decision date, reviewer and later corrections for every future assessment.

## Principles for any future SDC mechanism

1. **Define the unit before use.** Specify exactly what physical outcome or modeled risk reduction one unit represents, its time and orbital context, and what evidence supports it. A generic “one debris credit” must not be presented as equivalent to a tonne of carbon.
2. **Establish a defensible baseline.** Explain what would likely have happened without the intervention and distinguish legal compliance from additional action. Test displacement and unintended effects.
3. **Independent verification.** Separate the claimant from verification and disclose verifier conflicts. Provide an audit trail appropriate to safety, privacy and security constraints.
4. **No double counting.** Assign unique identifiers and states for claims, approvals, issuance, transfer if ever permitted, retirement and correction. Prevent multiple claims for one outcome and report reversals or invalidation.
5. **Governance before markets.** Decide whether credits, deposits, bonds, fees, procurement criteria or producer-responsibility rules best serve the objective. Trading is one possible policy design; v0.1 does not assume it.
6. **Equal access.** Publish eligibility and review rules that apply equally to every participant, including any organisation affiliated with the originator. No proprietary implementation should be required to take part in the public method.
7. **Access for emerging space actors.** Debris rules must not become a barrier to entry. Any revenue the mechanism creates should fund a published share of support for States and organisations with fewer resources, governed separately from issuance. The Rulebook draft calls this the Equitable Space Access Fund.

## Governance proposed for the Institute

The Institute should publish method changes, conflicts, funding influences, reviewer selection, evidence thresholds, complaint handling, corrections and appeal decisions. An independent expert group should challenge the method and publish reasons for accepting or rejecting substantive changes. A person or organization involved in a claim must recuse itself from the relevant assessment or appeal. Official names and marks need separately published use rules; a document license is not a certification license.

## Public consultation questions

- Which exposure dimensions are measurable well enough to compare missions, and which should remain descriptive?
- What evidence can be public without compromising legitimate operator confidentiality or security?
- How should missing data change a provisional assessment?
- What baseline and unit, if any, could make a cleanup or risk-reduction claim hold across different orbits and timescales?
- Which policy instrument would create useful incentives without letting a payment excuse avoidable harm?
- What institutional structure gives operators a fair appeal while preserving independence from any commercial implementer?

## Scope and provenance

This v0.1 text expresses a proposal for consultation. It specifies no scoring formula, data schema, issuance process, registry, conformity test or market. Any later operational method requires published research, validation, independent governance and a separate release decision.
