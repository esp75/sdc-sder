# A fairer way to take responsibility for space debris

**Status:** draft public explainer for coordinated release; v0.1 · 27 September 2026.  
**License:** © 2026 Maxime Kadri. Licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

Satellites help us communicate, navigate and understand Earth. When a mission ends, the object does not simply disappear. The question is: **how can we see who is managing the risk left behind, and recognize actions that genuinely reduce it?**

The proposed framework has two parts:

1. **SDER is the report card idea.** A Space Debris Exposure Rating would use a public method to explain a mission's debris exposure and how it manages its full lifecycle. The method must show what is known, what is uncertain and what evidence is missing. There is no official SDER score yet.
2. **SDC is the verified-action idea.** A Space Debris Credit could one day record a cleanup or risk-reduction outcome that an independent body has checked. Before any unit exists, researchers must define what counts, what would have happened otherwise and how to prevent the same outcome being claimed twice. No SDC is currently issued or tradable.

Think of the proposed SDC safeguards as **similar to carbon-credit safeguards**: define a claim, check the evidence and record it so it cannot be counted twice. The analogy stops there. Orbital risk is not measured in tonnes of carbon; effects differ by orbit, object and time. A payment would not make an unsafe mission acceptable. Carbon-credit programs themselves face integrity challenges, which is why [independent verification, transparent tracking and double-counting controls](https://icvcm.org/core-carbon-principles/) matter.

**A simple example:** A mission says it will safely dispose of its satellite, then later supplies evidence of what actually happened. A future SDER assessment might compare the plan and outcome while showing uncertainty. If a separate cleanup removes an abandoned object, a future SDC method would have to verify the result before recognizing any credit. Today, both are research proposals, not official decisions.

## 90-second spoken version

“Satellites connect our world, but a mission can leave physical risks in orbit. Right now, it is hard for the public to see how different missions manage that responsibility. Maxime Kadri originated the SDC and SDER ideas. The SDC Institute proposes to develop them as an openly licensed, independently governed framework. SDER would be a way to explain a mission's debris exposure using evidence and honest uncertainty. SDC could one day recognize a cleanup or risk reduction, but only after the result and the rules are independently checked. The carbon-credit system offers one useful comparison: define a claim, verify it and avoid counting it twice. Space debris is different from carbon, so the measurement and policy must be built for orbit. No official score or credit exists yet. We are opening the principles now so scientists, agencies, operators and the public can challenge them before they become a rule.”

## What happens next?

Read the [v0.1 principles](../../spec/vision-and-principles-v0.1.md), ask which evidence is missing, and suggest safeguards. Sharing the explainer does not endorse an operator, rating or credit. The SDC Institute's role is to develop an independent method, including corrections and appeals, before any official assessment.
